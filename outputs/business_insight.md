# Customer Segmentation — Business Insight & Action Plan

## Methodology

RFM analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) was applied to 2,500 households from the
Dunnhumby Complete Journey dataset. Three clustering algorithms were evaluated:

| Method       | Best k | Silhouette Score | Notes                                         |
|--------------|--------|------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| KMeans       | 2      | 0.656            | Clean but only discovers Active / Churned. Missed VIPs. |
| Hierarchical | 3      | 0.545            | Finds similar 3-segment structure, weaker separation |
| DBSCAN       | 3      | 0.758            | Best score, discovers VIP outliers automatically |

**Data Standardization & Export:**
To enable a side-by-side comparison across all algorithms, the disparate cluster labels generated by the models were mapped into a **Unified Segment System**:
* **`0` = VIP** (High Spend, High Frequency)
* **`1` = Active Shoppers** (Average Spend/Frequency)
* **`2` = Churned** (High Recency, Low Spend/Frequency)

The final exported dataset (`All_Customer_Segments_Combined.csv`) retains the `CustomerID` as the primary key, converts all financial metrics to clean integers, and includes the unified labels for all three models.

Both Hierarchical and DBSCAN independently agreed on 3 segments, validating the cluster structure. DBSCAN was selected as the final baseline model due to its highest silhouette score and its ability to detect the high-value VIP segment without requiring k to be specified in advance.

The dendrogram (Ward linkage) confirms k=3 as the natural cut point. Three distinct branches are visible below the distance threshold of 10.

## Action Plan per Segment

### 🏆 VIP (55 customers)
These 55 customers generate **3x the spend** of an average Active Shopper.
Their top categories include Soft Drinks, Beef, and Domestic Wine, which is a notably
premium basket compared to regular customers.

**Action:** Launch a **VIP loyalty program** with exclusive early access,
personalized offers on their top categories, and dedicated retention incentives.
Losing even one VIP customer costs the business ~$10,000 in annual revenue.

### 🛒 Active Shoppers (2,431 customers)
The core customer base: they shop every 22 days with consistent spend.

**Action:** **Retention via personalized promotions** on their top categories
(Soft Drinks, Beef, Cheese). Focus on increasing basket size rather than visit
frequency, which is already high.

### ⚠️ Churned (14 customers)
Haven't shopped in ~544 days. Their historical categories (Beef, Beers/Ales,
Chicken) differ slightly from Active Shoppers, suggesting a distinct profile.

**Action:** **Re-engagement campaign** with coupons targeted at their last
known purchase categories. Recovering 50% of churned customers at their
historical spend recovers ~**$1,140 in annual revenue**.

---

## Key Takeaway

> Without segmentation, all 2,500 customers would receive the same promotion, which means
> wasting budget on Active Shoppers who already shop regularly and missing the
> opportunity to retain VIPs and re-engage churned customers with personalized offers.
> Three independent clustering methods converge on the same segment structure,
> giving high confidence that these groups reflect real behavioral differences
> in the customer base.
